Actuarial science · Mathematical statistics · Mathematical modelling

Modelling longevity, catastrophe risk and insurance benefits and sharing risk

I am interested in theoretical and applied research across actuarial science, mathematical statistics and mathematical modelling. My current focus is on machine learning and time series methods (ARIMA, GARCH and FIGARCH) for global longevity forecasting; stochastic pricing, valuation and tail-risk assessment of life insurance benefits with embedded guarantees; catastrophe risk modelling, including extreme-value and frequency–severity methods for natural catastrophe losses; and optimal (re)insurance contracts and risk sharing.

Research

01

Machine learning for global longevity forecasting

Forecasting mortality and life expectancy across many populations using machine learning methods that capture shared trends and cross-country patterns.

02

Time series models of mortality

Applying ARIMA, GARCH and FIGARCH models to mortality data to forecast longevity trends and quantify uncertainty in those forecasts.

03

Catastrophe risk modelling

Modelling natural catastrophe losses using extreme-value theory and frequency–severity methods to quantify tail risk for insurers and reinsurers.

04

Optimal (re)insurance contracts

Designing insurance and reinsurance contracts that allocate risk efficiently between insurers, reinsurers and policyholders.

05

Risk sharing and risk transfer

Studying how longevity, catastrophe and other insurance risks can be shared and transferred fairly among participants in a risk pool.

About

I trained as an actuary at KNUST, graduating with a First Class BSc in Actuarial Science, then completed an MSc in Mathematical Sciences at AIMS Ghana and an MPhil in Mathematical Statistics at KNUST, where I also worked as a teaching and research assistant. As a Mastercard Foundation Scholar, I completed the Master of Financial Insurance at the University of Toronto.

Alongside my studies I have worked in life insurance at Prudential Life Insurance Ghana, on pricing models, cash flow reconciliation and IFRS 17 liabilities, and taught secondary school mathematics. My research interests are machine learning and time series methods (ARIMA, GARCH and FIGARCH) for global longevity forecasting, and optimal (re)insurance contracts and risk sharing.

Good actuarial research should be mathematically rigorous and useful for the decisions insurers and societies face.
